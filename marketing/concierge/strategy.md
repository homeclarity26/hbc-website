## 1. Executive Summary

Adam's instinct on this offering is right. The market is real, the local field is wide open, and the pricing band of $199 to $550 per month is inside what comparable national operators charge.

**The economics work because HBC is selling management, not service delivery.** Vendor invoices pass through to the homeowner at exact cost. The monthly fee is pure compensation for coordination, vetting, scheduling, the client portal, the annual home assessment, the maintenance calendar, and one accountable point of contact. That structure flips the model from a thin-margin service bundler into a high-margin coordination business with software-like contribution margins.

Recommended structure for approval before any page work begins:

| Tier | Price | What it really is |
|---|---|---|
| **Essentials** | $199/mo | Coordination of core outdoor and safety services. Lawn, snow, gutters, pest, annual safety check. Vendors paid through HBC at exact cost. |
| **Comfort** | $350/mo | Coordination of the full seasonal calendar. Adds lawn treatment, leaves, HVAC tune-ups, sprinklers, chimney and dryer vent, water heater flush, roof and plumbing visual checks. House cleaning is NOT included; it is the most popular add-on. |
| **Premier** | $550/mo | Comfort plus full property management: mulching, shrub trim, window cleaning, alternating-year power washing, monthly house cleaning (capped at 2,500 sqft), one handyman half-day per year, named account manager. |
| **Snowbird** | $399/mo (Nov-Apr only, 6-month commit) | Bi-weekly home walk-throughs with photo report, mail and package handling, snow plowing, HVAC monitoring, freeze-event response, 24/7 dispatch, spring-return prep. |

At these prices the model produces 65 to 73% contribution margins per customer, LTV-to-CAC ratios of 25x or better at every tier, and a roughly $600K cumulative 3-year contribution under the base scenario in `profitability-model.xlsx`. The lot-size supplements ($25 / $45 per month) cover the operational complexity of larger properties without changing the core price points.

The single most important thing to internalize before approving copy: **the customer is not buying lawn care. They are buying their weekends back, their mental load gone, and one number to call when something goes wrong.** Every page word, every channel, every onboarding interaction should reinforce that. The fee buys peace of mind, not vendor labor.

## 2. Strategic Fit: Why This Offering Belongs to HBC

**The North Star says this is the right move.** Adam's stated North Star is "find me first, before they find a realtor, GC, architect, or designer." A one-time remodel, even a $150K kitchen, is a transactional touchpoint. A monthly concierge subscription is a year-round, in-the-home, trusted relationship. It is the most direct mechanism HBC has to *be* the homeowner's first call.

**It compounds the rest of the HBC product line.** A subscriber who has lived inside the HBC vendor network for 18 months is the warmest possible buyer for a Home Clarity Report ($4,500). A homeowner whose gutters are cleaned twice a year by HBC's preferred sub is already inside the trust loop when they decide to remodel a kitchen, and AK Renovations gets that referral.

**It generates recurring revenue that smooths the remodel pipeline.** AK Renovations is project-based, lumpy, and seasonally variable. Concierge revenue is monthly, predictable, and counter-cyclical to remodel demand. Recurring services are more in demand when discretionary projects slow down.

**It is positioned correctly only if it is HBC, not AK.** This is critical and cannot be compromised on. The moment a homeowner thinks the concierge service exists to upsell a remodel, the trust is gone. HBC stays as the brand. AK is the *outcome* of trust, referred only when the homeowner asks about remodeling. The concierge page never mentions AK Renovations.

## 3. Target Customer

### Primary persona: the buyer the page must speak to first

**The Overloaded Partner.** Dual-income household, both working full-time, kids at home, household income $200K+, 3,000 to 4,500 sqft home in Hudson, Bath, Montrose-Ghent, or Richfield. One spouse is the de facto "house manager" and resents it. They already spend an estimated $8,800 to $10,800 per year on outsourced home services in fragments. The pain is not money. It is the mental tax of remembering when gutters were last cleaned, why the lawn guy stopped showing up, and which HVAC company isn't going to ghost them.

This persona is roughly **30% to 35% of the buyer pool** and the highest-volume, fastest-converting segment.

### Secondary personas: high LTV, lower volume

- **The Empty Nester (55 to 70).** Bath Township, Hudson, Montrose-Ghent. House paid off, kids gone. Income is fine; energy and tolerance for managing contractors is low. Sticky once acquired, low churn. About 25% to 30% of buyers. Best entered through realtor or country-club referral, not paid digital.
- **The Snowbird (60 to 75).** Florida or Arizona Nov to Apr. Currently paying neighbors, family, or one-off "home watch" services to check the house. Willing to pay $400+/mo for real peace of mind. About 15% to 20% of buyers and the *highest* LTV per dollar of service cost.
- **The Recent Transplant.** Just moved in from out of state. No vendor network. Will pay starter-tier prices to skip 18 months of trial-and-error vendor sourcing. About 5% to 10% of buyers.

### Geographic concentration

[Hudson 44236 has 4,100 to 4,600 households earning $150K+ with home values above $400K](https://www.neilsberg.com/insights/hudson-oh-median-household-income/). Bath Township adds another 1,300 to 1,500. Montrose-Ghent CDP adds 1,000 to 1,100. Together, the three core ZIPs hold roughly **6,500 to 7,200 qualifying households** before secondary markets. At 5% three-year penetration, that is roughly 325 to 360 members and $1.5M to $2M in ARR from core geography alone.

### What the buyer actually buys (jobs-to-be-done)

1. **Time recovery.** "I get my Saturdays back."
2. **Mental load elimination.** "I don't have to remember when the gutters need cleaning."
3. **One number to call.** "If something goes wrong, I call HBC, not five different companies."
4. **Vetted, accountable vendors.** "I don't have to vet anyone. Adam already did."
5. **Snowbird peace of mind.** "Someone is checking my house every two weeks while I'm in Naples."
6. **Home value protection.** "Everything that should be done is being done, on time."

These are emotional jobs, not functional ones. Copy that leads with service lists fails. Copy that leads with the emotional outcome and *then* substantiates with service lists wins.

## 4. Pricing Tier Rationale: Final Recommendation

### The economic model

HBC charges a pure management fee. Vendor invoices flow through HBC to the homeowner at exact cost (cost plus 0%). The monthly fee compensates HBC for coordination, vetting, scheduling, payment handling, the client portal, the annual home assessment, and the maintenance calendar. This is the same structure used by [Honey Homes](https://www.honeyhomes.com), Setter (acquired by Thumbtack), and [Executive Home Management](https://www.executivehomemanagement.com) in Cleveland. It is dramatically more profitable than service-bundling because HBC carries no vendor cost on its P&L.

Detailed numbers are in `profitability-model.xlsx`. The per-customer annual economics summary:

| Tier | Monthly fee | Annual HBC revenue (incl. add-ons) | HBC direct cost | Contribution margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Essentials | $199 | $2,568 | $696 | $1,872 (73%) |
| Comfort | $350 | $4,560 | $1,236 | $3,324 (73%) |
| Premier | $550 | $7,200 | $1,956 | $5,244 (73%) |
| Snowbird | $399 (6 mo) | $2,514 | $888 | $1,626 (65%) |

"Direct cost" here is HBC's own cost: Adam's coordination time, software, insurance, and the cost-of-goods on add-on sales. It does NOT include vendor invoices, which the homeowner pays separately at exact cost.

Lot-size supplements ($25 / $45 per month for half-acre and 3/4-to-1 acre lots) cover the additional coordination time these properties require without re-pricing the core tiers. They also align HBC's revenue with the larger pass-through volume those homes generate.

### Why this is the right structure

**Margins compound at higher tiers.** Premier costs Adam 20 hours per year of coordination time versus 6 hours for Essentials, but generates 2.8x the revenue. The model rewards Adam for taking on the higher-touch customers who also have the highest LTV.

**House cleaning is no longer an economic landmine.** Because HBC does not carry the cost of cleaning in the fee, including it in Premier is just additional coordination. The homeowner pays the cleaner directly through HBC at the cleaner's rate. HBC earns no margin on the cleaning itself, only the management fee. This is structurally cleaner and lets Premier deliver a much fuller package without margin pressure.

**Vendor pass-through is transparent and trust-building.** Members see exactly what each vendor charges. There is no hidden markup, no surprise invoice, no "we marked this up 30%." The trust this creates is itself part of why members renew.

### The recommended structure

The names and prices below preserve Adam's $199/$350/$550 anchors and add the Snowbird product the research strongly indicates is the single biggest LTV opportunity HBC is currently leaving on the table.

**Tagline:** *"The five things every Hudson homeowner should never have to think about."*

**Five included services:**

| Service | Scope | Annual cap |

## 5. What Counts as "One Service": The Cap Logic

This is the rule the page must publish clearly so a $199 customer cannot logic their way into $5,000 of bundled service.

**Rule 1.** A "service" is a named line item with a published annual occurrence cap. Lawn mowing equals 1 service equals 26 mows. Snow plowing equals 1 service equals 20 pushes. The cap is the cap; events beyond it are billed at the published add-on rate.

**Rule 2.** Ancillary tasks within a named service are included; scope-expansion tasks are not. Edging and blowing are part of "lawn mowing." Aeration, dethatching, overseeding, and sod repair are *not*. They are separately quoted.

**Rule 3.** On-demand services are never included. Handyman, plumbing, electrical, and tree work are always at preferred-vendor rates with the member discount applied. The discount is the benefit.

**Rule 4.** Emergency dispatch is coordination only. HBC dispatches the vendor within agreed response windows at no additional charge. Trade labor and parts are billed by the vendor at preferred rates.

**Rule 5.** Out-of-scope work is quoted, not assumed. If a request falls outside the published scope of a named service, HBC provides a written quote before the work happens. The concierge has authority to grant one no-charge "goodwill" exception per member per year.

**Published member-agreement language:**

> *"Each named service in your plan is defined by its standard scope and annual occurrence limit as published in the Service Catalog. Visiting your home does not entitle you to any service beyond the defined scope. HBC will notify you before any out-of-scope work is performed and provide pricing in advance."*

## 6. What Is NOT Included (Always Quoted Separately)

This is the critical expectation-setting list. Publish on the page. The most common cancellation reason in concierge subscriptions is *misunderstood scope*.

- Major plumbing, electrical, or HVAC repairs (any repair estimated above $500)
- Roof replacement or structural repair
- Mold remediation
- Interior painting (full walls or rooms; handyman touch-up only)
- Landscaping design, new planting beds, hardscaping
- Tree removal (separate hazard assessment required)
- Pool opening or closing (specialized sub required)
- Driveway replacement or major crack repair
- Appliance purchase or installation
- Any work requiring permits (permit fees and licensed-trade fees billed to the homeowner)
- Custom or unusual requests outside the published service catalog

## 7. Vendor Management Framework

**Vendor recruitment.** HBC recruits 2 to 3 preferred vendors per service category in the core geography. Recruitment script and agreement key terms are in `operations-playbook.md` (next deliverable, after pricing approval).

**Vendor agreement key terms (preview; final draft in playbook):**

- Preferred-rate pricing 15% to 25% below the vendor's retail (volume justified by route density and predictable monthly billings)
- Net-15 payment from HBC, weekly or biweekly cycle (faster than industry standard; this is part of how HBC earns the discount)
- Insurance minimums verified and on file
- Quality SLAs: arrival window honored, post-visit photo, customer feedback survey
- Non-poach: vendor cannot solicit HBC members directly during the agreement and for 12 months after
- Non-exclusive on the vendor's side (HBC is not asking them to give up their book)

**Vendor scorecard.** Quarterly review on five metrics:

1. On-time arrival rate
2. Customer survey score (post-visit 1 to 5 rating)
3. Photo and documentation compliance
4. Communication responsiveness (HBC concierge to vendor)
5. Quality complaint rate

Two consecutive quarters below threshold means the vendor moves to backup status. Three consecutive means removed from preferred list.

**Vendor concentration risk.** No single vendor in any category should exceed 60% of HBC's volume in that category. Maintain at least one fully-vetted backup per service.

## 8. Customer Onboarding (First 30 Days)

What happens when somebody signs up determines whether they stay 18 months or 3 months.

**Day 0, signup.** Stripe subscription created. Welcome email with onboarding scheduler link, first-month service calendar preview, concierge contact info.

**Days 1 to 3, onboarding call (15 min).** Adam or the concierge manager personally calls the new member. Confirms property details, lot size, key access, gate codes, pets, special considerations. Sets expectations on the first month's scheduled services.

**Days 4 to 10, in-home walk-through.** A scheduled visit, paired with the first scheduled service when possible, where HBC walks the property, photographs each service area, notes condition, and produces the **Home Profile**, a one-page document the member receives within 5 days.

**Days 11 to 30, first services delivered.** Each completed service produces a photo and a one-line note in the member portal. The first 30 days establish the pattern: service happens, member is told it happened, member sees proof.

**Day 30, first check-in.** Concierge text or call: "How are we doing? Anything you want different?" This single touchpoint is the highest-impact retention action in the entire program.

## 9. Service Request Flow

**Channels:** Text (primary), email, phone, member portal. Adam's existing 330-203-1331 line is the published number. After-hours emergency calls roll to the on-call dispatcher.

**SLA targets:**

- Routine request: response within 2 hours, business hours
- Same-week service: confirm vendor and schedule within 24 hours
- Emergency (burst pipe, no heat, sewer): vendor dispatched within 2 hours; Premier members within 90 minutes

**Member portal.** Phase 1: a simple branded form in the existing HBC site for service requests, plus Stripe customer portal for billing. Phase 2 (3 to 6 months in): a member dashboard showing scheduled services, completed services with photos, account manager contact, and one-tap request button. Phase 1 is fine for launch.

## 10. Cancellation and Pause Policy

**Standard plans (Essentials, Comfort, Premier):**

- 30-day cancellation notice, no fee
- Seasonal pause available once per year up to 3 months (for example, extended travel); billing paused, services suspended
- Pro-rata refund for prepaid annual subscriptions cancelled mid-term, less the value of services delivered to date

**Snowbird:**

- Six-month commitment (Nov to Apr); cancellation before season end forfeits remaining months
- Renewal in October each year is opt-in; auto-prompt sent September 1

**Why this matters:** Easy cancellation is a positive signal. The objection "what if I can't cancel" is one of the top five objections in the research. Make cancellation visible on the page and friction-free in practice.

## 11. Branding and Naming

**Recommendation: HBC Concierge.**

Rationale:

- Keeps the offering inside the HBC brand. No sub-brand to build, no separate domain to maintain.
- The word "concierge" is intuitive and on-trend in this category. Honey Homes uses "home upkeep," Setter used "home manager," Executive Home Management uses "home concierge."
- The phrase "HBC Concierge" reads naturally in a sentence ("I subscribe to HBC Concierge") and in a search query.
- Differentiates clearly from AK Renovations remodel work. The word "concierge" anchors the service-management identity.

Rejected alternatives:

- *"The Hub Membership"*: too inside-baseball, doesn't tell a stranger what the product does.
- *"HBC Home Services"*: too generic, sounds like a service line, not a membership.
- *Standalone sub-brand*: splits brand equity HBC is already building, adds maintenance overhead, no upside.

**URL recommendation: `/concierge`.** Short, clean, matches the brand language, easy to say on the phone ("hometownbuildersclub.com/concierge"). Alternatives `/membership` and `/home-services-membership` are weaker. `/membership` is too generic (membership in what?), and the longer URL is harder to share verbally.

## 12. Connection to Home Clarity Report

This needs to be subtle. The page mentions the Report exists; it does not require the Report; it does not lead with it.

**Recommended treatment:**

- A single section titled something like *"Already have a Home Clarity Report? Your concierge plan picks up where it left off."* One paragraph, light footer styling, links to the Report page.
- Members who own a Report get a $50 credit toward their first three months of concierge service (acquisition incentive, low cost).
- Members who subscribe for 12 months get $500 off a Report if they buy one (retention plus cross-sell).

**What NOT to do:** Do not require the Report to subscribe. Do not lead the page with "first you need a Report." That creates a $4,500 gate before a $199 subscription, killing conversion. The Report is a *deepening* product for members who want documentation; the concierge is the relationship.

## 13. The Anti-Goals (Things This Offering Is Not)

Worth being explicit about so the page doesn't drift into them:

- **It is not a home warranty.** Warranties cover catastrophic system breakdowns. HBC manages routine maintenance. We coordinate emergency repairs but the homeowner pays the trade.
- **It is not a marketplace.** TaskRabbit, Angi, and Thumbtack are lead-gen. The homeowner still does the work. HBC does the work *for* the homeowner.
- **It is not a remodel sales funnel.** The page never mentions AK Renovations. Subscribers who later need remodel work get referred, but the concierge offering must stand on its own.
- **It is not "any handyman whenever."** Honey Homes is handyman-only with a visit bank. HBC is service-bundled with named scopes. Different product.
