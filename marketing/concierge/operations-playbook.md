# HBC Concierge Operations Playbook

Adam Kilgore, Hometown Builders Club
Cuyahoga Falls, Ohio
Version 1.0, May 2026

This playbook is the operational manual for running HBC Concierge as a management-fee business in Summit County, Ohio. It assumes the strategy doc and profitability model have been read. It covers the entire workflow from vendor recruitment through monthly billing, with the actual scripts, agreements, and SOPs Adam needs to run the offering.

The model in two sentences: HBC charges a flat monthly fee to coordinate every home service vendor on the homeowner's behalf. Vendor invoices are passed to the homeowner at exact cost, with HBC making zero markup on services.

---

## Section 1. The Operational Truth Table

Before any vendor is recruited or any member is signed, the entire team needs to internalize five rules. If anyone on the HBC side gets these wrong, the model breaks.

| # | Rule | Why it matters |
|---|------|----------------|
| 1 | HBC never marks up a vendor invoice. Pass-through is exact-cost. | This is the trust foundation. One markup discovered ends the business. |
| 2 | HBC never accepts a kickback or commission from a vendor. | Same reason. Vendors will offer. The answer is always no. |
| 3 | The monthly fee is the full revenue from the member. Adam's coordination time is paid for inside that fee. | If Adam thinks he should be billing hourly on top, the model collapses. |
| 4 | The member pays HBC. HBC pays the vendor. Never reverse this flow. | If members pay vendors directly, HBC has no way to enforce quality. |
| 5 | Every vendor signs the HBC vendor agreement before doing one job for a member. | The agreement is what makes pass-through legal and clean. |

These five rules belong on a printed card on Adam's desk.

## Section 2. Vendor Recruitment

### 2.1 Target vendor categories at launch

The first cohort of members needs coverage in these categories. Adam should aim for two vetted vendors per category before public launch, three for high-frequency categories.

| Category | Vendors needed | Service frequency |
|---|---|---|
| House cleaning | 3 | Weekly to monthly |
| Lawn care, mowing | 3 | Weekly May to October |
| Snow removal | 2 | Per-event Dec to March |
| HVAC tune and repair | 2 | Twice yearly plus emergency |
| Plumbing | 2 | On-demand |
| Electrical | 2 | On-demand |
| Handyman, general | 3 | Weekly to monthly |
| Gutter cleaning | 1 | Twice yearly |
| Pest control | 1 | Quarterly |
| Window cleaning | 1 | Twice yearly |
| Tree care | 1 | Annual or storm |
| Painter, interior | 1 | On-demand |
| Pool service | 1 (if applicable) | Weekly seasonal |

### 2.2 Vendor sourcing channels

In order of preference, Adam pulls from:

1. The existing AK Renovations subcontractor network. These are people Adam has worked with for years. About 60 percent of the launch vendor list will come from here.
2. The Home Clarity Report vendor database. These are vendors already vetted on HCR projects.
3. Direct referrals from existing trusted vendors. Ask each recruited vendor who they recommend in adjacent trades.
4. Word of mouth from realtor partners who work in the Summit County premium home market.
5. Last resort: cold outreach to highly rated companies on Google and BBB. Vet hard.

### 2.3 The recruitment conversation script

This is the script for the first call with a candidate vendor. Adam, or whoever is recruiting, leads it.

Opening, in plain language:

"Hey, this is Adam from HBC. I'm building a small home concierge service in Summit County, just members in Hudson, Bath, Fairlawn, that area. I'm looking for a [trade] partner. Quick pitch. Have a minute?"

The pitch:

"Members pay HBC monthly. They text us when they need things. We dispatch you to their home. You bill HBC at your normal rate. No markup, no commission, no kickback, you charge what you charge. We pay you on standard net-15 terms once we're billed by you, and we collect from the member separately. The win for you is we send you steady, qualified work in a tight geography. The members are upper-middle to high-income, they pay for quality, they don't haggle. You don't deal with the homeowner directly on scheduling or billing, that all comes through us."

Then the qualification questions, in order:

1. "Are you fully licensed and insured? I'll need a copy of your COI before we send anyone." (No COI, no deal.)
2. "What's your typical response time when a member texts in?" (Looking for 24 to 48 hours.)
3. "How are you priced? Hourly, flat rate, by the job?" (You need to understand their pricing structure.)
4. "What's your service radius? Will you go to Hudson, Bath, Fairlawn, Richfield, Stow, Cuyahoga Falls, Copley, Tallmadge, Peninsula?" (At least three of these is required.)
5. "Are you ok with us paying you and the member never seeing your invoice directly? You'd invoice HBC, we pay you, we bill the member exactly what you charged."
6. "Can we start with a one-month trial period where we send you two or three jobs and see how it goes?"

If they're a fit, send the vendor agreement same day. If not, end politely and move on.

### 2.4 Vendor vetting checklist

Before any vendor does work for a member:

- [ ] Active business registration verified, Ohio Secretary of State
- [ ] Liability insurance certificate obtained, minimum $1M, HBC named as additional insured
- [ ] Workers' comp coverage verified if they have employees
- [ ] License verified if the trade requires one (electrical, plumbing, HVAC)
- [ ] Three reference checks completed, ideally from existing relationships
- [ ] Vendor agreement signed and on file
- [ ] W9 received for 1099 reporting
- [ ] Banking info captured for ACH payment
- [ ] First-job trial completed, member feedback positive

A vendor is not added to the active dispatch list until every box is checked.

## Section 3. The Vendor Agreement

This is the plain-language agreement HBC has every vendor sign. Adam should have his attorney review and finalize, but this captures the operational structure.

### Hometown Builders Club Vendor Service Agreement

This agreement is between Hometown Builders Club LLC (HBC) and [VENDOR NAME] (Vendor).

**1. Purpose.** HBC operates a homeowner concierge service. HBC dispatches Vendor to perform services at the homes of HBC members, on terms described below.

**2. Pricing.** Vendor will charge HBC the same rate Vendor would charge the homeowner directly. Vendor will not inflate pricing for HBC referrals, and will not offer HBC discounts in exchange for volume that result in pricing below what Vendor charges the public. Vendor invoices HBC. HBC pays Vendor.

**3. No commission, kickback, or rebate.** Neither party will offer or accept any commission, kickback, rebate, or other financial inducement based on referral volume. HBC's revenue is the monthly fee paid by members. Vendor's revenue is the work performed. The two are independent.

**4. Pass-through to member.** HBC bills the member exactly what Vendor charges HBC. Member sees a single consolidated bill from HBC. Vendor does not bill member directly.

**5. Insurance.** Vendor maintains liability insurance of not less than $1,000,000 per occurrence, with HBC named as additional insured. Vendor provides current COI on signing and at each renewal.

**6. Quality standard.** Vendor will deliver work meeting the quality standards represented during recruitment. Repeated quality failures or member complaints are grounds for HBC to remove Vendor from active dispatch.

**7. Scheduling.** Member service requests come from HBC, not directly from members. Vendor confirms scheduling with HBC. Vendor does not market additional services directly to members without HBC consent.

**8. Payment terms.** HBC pays Vendor within 15 days of receiving Vendor invoice, by ACH or check. HBC has the right to withhold payment on disputed invoices pending resolution.

**9. Independent contractor.** Vendor is an independent contractor, not an employee or agent of HBC. Vendor is responsible for own taxes, withholdings, and benefits.

**10. Termination.** Either party may terminate this agreement with 30 days written notice. HBC may terminate immediately for cause, including but not limited to insurance lapse, quality failure, or breach of this agreement.

**11. Confidentiality.** Vendor will treat member identity, address, and home details as confidential. Vendor will not share member information with third parties.

Signatures, both parties.

### Why these 11 clauses matter

Clauses 2 and 3 are the structural firewall that protects HBC's pass-through promise. Clause 4 protects the billing flow. Clauses 5 and 11 protect HBC and members. Clause 7 prevents vendors from poaching members. Clause 6 gives HBC the right to fire bad vendors without legal friction.

## Section 4. Member Onboarding SOP

Once a member signs up, the onboarding is a fixed 30-day process.

### Day 0, signup day

- Member completes signup form on /concierge contact form
- HBC receives notification, Adam responds within 4 business hours
- 20-minute fit call scheduled within 5 days

### Day 1 to 5, fit call

- Adam runs the 20-minute fit call by phone or video
- Confirms address is in service area
- Walks through current vendor situation (who cleans, who mows, who plows)
- Identifies pain points (vendor turnover, missed appointments, billing chaos)
- Recommends a tier
- If member commits, sends member agreement and ACH authorization for monthly billing
- Member signs both within 48 hours

### Day 6 to 10, onboarding visit

- Adam visits the home in person, 60 to 90 minutes
- Walks the property: exterior, mechanicals, finishes, recent and pending work
- Photographs key items and writes notes (if member is a Home Clarity Report owner, this is reduced because the data already exists)
- Documents existing vendor list, contact info, scheduling cadence
- Identifies gaps and opportunities for vendor replacement
- Sets up member portal account
- Confirms communication preferences (text, email, both)
- Schedules first proactive services

### Day 11 to 20, vendor handoff

- Adam contacts every existing vendor on member's list
- Notifies them: "Going forward, scheduling and billing for [member name] runs through HBC. Here's how it works."
- For vendors willing to bill HBC, they stay in member's rotation
- For vendors who refuse or who are underperforming, Adam offers a vetted replacement
- Member is informed of any changes before they happen

### Day 21 to 30, run mode

- First HBC-coordinated service is dispatched
- Member portal is populated with first vendor invoice and payment
- Member receives first consolidated monthly bill from HBC
- Adam confirms with member at day 30 that the system is running
- Quarterly check-in scheduled for ~day 90

### Onboarding deliverables (member-facing)

By day 30, every member has:

1. A signed member agreement
2. Active monthly ACH billing
3. A member portal login with vendor list, schedule, and document vault
4. A written maintenance calendar for the year ahead
5. A direct text line to HBC for any service request
6. A scheduled quarterly check-in

## Section 5. Service Request Handling

### 5.1 The text-in workflow

Members text or email a designated HBC inbox. The workflow is:

1. **Receive.** Member texts the HBC line. Auto-confirmation goes out within minutes: "Got it. Dispatching now."
2. **Triage.** Adam (or CSM after hire) classifies: routine recurring, on-demand new, urgent, or emergency.
3. **Dispatch.** For routine and on-demand, dispatch to the appropriate vendor with a target window.
4. **Confirm.** Once vendor confirms time, member is updated.
5. **Verify.** After service, vendor sends invoice to HBC. HBC verifies completion (photo, member confirmation, or vendor report).
6. **Pay.** HBC pays vendor within 15 days. Member is billed in next monthly cycle at exact pass-through cost.

### 5.2 Response-time SLA by tier

| Tier | Routine response | Urgent response | Emergency response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Essentials | 24 business hours | 8 business hours | Same day handoff to emergency vendor |
| Comfort | 24 business hours | 8 business hours | Same day handoff to emergency vendor |
| Premier | 4 business hours | 2 business hours | Same day handoff with Adam personal involvement |
| Snowbird | 24 business hours | 8 business hours | Same day with property check |

"Emergency" means active flooding, no heat in winter, no power, structural risk. These get a phone call from HBC, not a text.

### 5.3 The dispatch decision tree

When a member texts a request:

- **Is the home in service area?** If yes, continue. If no, this should never happen.
- **Is it a covered service category?** If yes, dispatch. If no, route to add-on or quote.
- **Is there a preferred vendor in member's history?** If yes, default to them. If they're unavailable, pick next-best from active list.
- **Does the request require Adam personally?** Project advisory, second opinions on bids, scope decisions, design questions. Adam handles these directly.

### 5.4 Add-on services

Members can request anything outside the core tier. HBC quotes the work, member approves, HBC dispatches and bills at exact vendor cost. No coordination markup on add-ons either, the relationship stays clean.

Common add-ons: holiday lighting, deep clean before guests, storage organization, painting, smart-home setup, appliance install. Add-ons usually run $200 to $2,000 per request.

## Section 6. Quality Control

### 6.1 The first 90 days with any vendor

Every new vendor gets a 90-day probation:

- First 3 jobs: HBC follows up with member within 48 hours of service
- Member rates the work, plain text feedback
- Adam reviews all probationary feedback weekly
- After 3 successful jobs, vendor moves to standard rotation

### 6.2 Standing quality routines

- Member text after each non-recurring service: "How did it go?"
- Quarterly check-ins include vendor satisfaction questions
- Annual review of vendor list, drop the bottom 10 percent
- Real-time complaint escalation, see 6.3

### 6.3 Complaint escalation flow

When a member complaint comes in:

1. Adam (or CSM) acknowledges within 4 hours
2. Vendor is contacted same day
3. Vendor proposes remedy: re-do, refund, partial credit
4. HBC mediates and gets member sign-off on remedy
5. If vendor refuses to remedy, vendor is removed from active list
6. Internal log of complaint and resolution maintained

### 6.4 Vendor performance metrics tracked

For every vendor, monthly:

- On-time arrival rate
- Member satisfaction average
- Complaint count
- Re-do rate
- Invoice accuracy (HBC against member-billed amount)

A vendor falling below 90 percent on-time or under 4.0/5.0 satisfaction average over a rolling 60 days gets a coaching call, then probation, then removal.

## Section 7. Billing Flow

### 7.1 The monthly cycle

| Day of month | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Member is billed for the upcoming month's management fee via ACH |
| 1 to 30 | Vendor invoices arrive throughout the month |
| 5 to 25 | Vendor invoices are paid by HBC, net-15 |
| 28 to 30 | Monthly statement is generated for each member, listing all pass-through vendor charges from prior month plus the management fee already collected |
| 1 of next month | Pass-through charges are auto-charged to member's ACH |

### 7.2 The single member statement

Every month, every member gets one statement showing:

- Management fee for the current month (Essentials $199, Comfort $350, etc., already paid)
- Pass-through services from the prior month, itemized:
  - Cleaning, [date], [vendor], $XXX
  - Lawn, [date], [vendor], $XXX
  - HVAC tune, [date], [vendor], $XXX
- Lot supplement if applicable
- Total pass-through charge for the month
- Year-to-date services log

This statement is the member's one piece of monthly paperwork. Clean, scannable, no surprises.

### 7.3 Software stack for billing

Recommended stack at launch:

- **Stripe** for ACH and card billing of management fees and pass-through charges
- **QuickBooks Online** for vendor payment, expense categorization, 1099 prep
- **Airtable or Notion** for member portal, vendor database, service log
- **Google Workspace** for email, calendar, document storage
- **Twilio or OpenPhone** for the dedicated HBC text line

Total software cost at launch: roughly $200 to $400 a month, scales to roughly $600 a month at 50 members.

### 7.4 Cash flow note

Because HBC pays vendors net-15 and bills members on day 1 of next month, HBC carries about two weeks of vendor payables before being reimbursed by member ACH. At 50 members, this float is roughly $5,000 to $8,000. Plan for it. Either Adam funds the working capital from existing cash, or HBC opens a $25,000 line of credit at Adam's bank to cover seasonal swings.

## Section 8. Member Communication Cadence

### 8.1 Inbound triggers (member to HBC)

Member texts in. HBC responds per SLA.

### 8.2 Outbound regular touchpoints

| Cadence | Touchpoint | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | Statement and "anything I should know about?" check-in text | Billing transparency, low-friction request |
| Quarterly | Scheduled 15-minute check-in call or in-person | Proactive planning, surface issues |
| Annual | In-home walkthrough with Adam | Update maintenance calendar, identify projects |
| Per-service | Post-service satisfaction text | Quality control |

### 8.3 Outbound emergency or proactive triggers

- Severe weather alert: HBC texts members in path with action plan
- Vendor failure: HBC alerts member same day
- Identified issue at home: HBC raises with member with recommended action

### 8.4 Tone

Plain, direct, owner-led. Adam writes like Adam talks. Texts are short. Calls are direct. No corporate hedging. Member should feel like they have a guy, not a vendor.

## Section 9. Software Stack Detail

### 9.3 Automations to build week one

1. New member signup form to Airtable to email Adam within 5 minutes

3. Vendor invoice received in Gmail to Airtable to QuickBooks bill creation

These five automations save Adam roughly 15 hours a month at 50 members.

## Section 10. Hiring and Scaling

### 10.1 Adam-only phase, 0 to 30 members

Adam handles everything: sales, onboarding, dispatch, vendor management, billing oversight. The model assumes 1 to 1.5 hours per member per month at this scale, plus 4 to 6 hours a week of admin.

Total Adam time at 30 members: roughly 60 to 75 hours a month. Manageable on top of HCR work because most member requests are quick text triages.

### 10.2 First hire trigger, 30 to 50 members

When member count reaches 30 to 35 sustained, hire a part-time CSM (Customer Success Manager). Profile:

- Local hire, ideally a homeowner in the service area
- Detail-oriented, comfortable with software, good written communication
- 20 to 25 hours a week to start
- Compensation: $25 to $30 an hour, $30K to $35K annualized

CSM duties:
- Triage and dispatch routine requests
- Manage vendor invoice flow
- Member statement generation
- Quarterly check-in scheduling and execution
- Adam still handles onboarding visits, complex requests, vendor recruitment

### 10.3 Full-time CSM at 50+, second hire at 75+

At 50 members, the part-time CSM moves to full-time at $45K to $55K loaded. At 75 members, second part-time CSM joins. At 100 members, the operational team is Adam plus 1.5 to 2 FTE.

### 10.4 What Adam keeps doing forever

Even at scale, Adam does these personally because they are the product:

- Onboarding visit for every new member
- Annual home walkthrough for every member
- Complex advisory calls (project decisions, contractor selection, scope debates)
- Vendor recruitment and termination decisions
- Quarterly business review

If Adam ever stops doing these, HBC Concierge becomes a generic dispatch service and the value collapses.

## Section 11. Risk Management

### 11.1 Insurance HBC carries

- General liability: $1M per occurrence, $2M aggregate. Required.
- Errors and omissions: $1M, recommended for advisory work.
- Cyber liability: $500K, recommended once member portal is live.

### 11.2 Vendor insurance verified

Every vendor maintains $1M minimum liability with HBC named as additional insured. COI on file, renewal calendar tracked.

### 11.3 Member agreement protections

The member agreement (separate document, attorney drafted) includes:

- HBC is a coordination service, not the performer of services
- Vendor is independent contractor, not HBC employee
- HBC will pursue remedies on member's behalf for vendor failure but does not guarantee vendor work
- Limitation of liability to fees paid in prior 12 months
- Mediation before litigation

### 11.4 Quality risk

The biggest non-financial risk is vendor failure damaging the HBC brand. Mitigation:
- Aggressive vetting at recruitment
- 90-day probation
- Member feedback after every service
- Fast removal of underperformers

### 11.5 Concentration risk

Do not let any single vendor represent more than 25 percent of HBC's vendor spend. If they go out of business or break the agreement, the disruption is contained.

## Section 13. The Things That Will Go Wrong

Every operational playbook is incomplete because reality breaks the plan. The most likely failure modes for HBC Concierge in year one, and the mitigation:

### Vendor refuses to bill HBC

Some smaller operators bill homeowners directly and don't want to add a third party to the loop. Mitigation: have 2 to 3 backup vendors per category. Be willing to walk.

### Member tries to negotiate the management fee down

The fee is the fee. Adam doesn't negotiate it. The member either pays $199, $350, or $550 (or $399 for Snowbird), or they don't sign up. Discount conversations happen only on the Home Clarity Report linkage credit, never on the fee itself.

### Member asks HBC to mark up a vendor invoice "to keep it simple"

Some members may try to consolidate all costs into one bigger HBC fee. Refuse. Pass-through pricing is the firewall. Once you mark up one invoice, you can't go back.

### Vendor asks for a kickback

Some vendors will offer 5 to 10 percent back for steady volume. Refuse, document the offer, and consider whether to keep the vendor at all. A vendor who tries to kick back is a vendor who'll cut corners on the work.

### Member churn in winter

Every month is the same fee. But homeowners feel the value differently in February versus July. Mitigation: visible value-add in low-season months (proactive maintenance reminders, written winter readiness reports, freeze-risk monitoring).

### Adam burns out

The model relies on Adam personally for the first 30 to 50 members. The CSM hire trigger is non-negotiable. Cross 35 sustained members, hire the CSM, even if cash is tight.

### A vendor causes property damage

It will happen eventually. Mitigation: insurance is the answer, vendor's first, HBC's second. Documented service log makes claims easier. Member agreement limits HBC's exposure to fees paid.

## Section 14. Quick-Reference Cards

### The 30-second member elevator pitch

"HBC Concierge is a monthly membership for homeowners who'd rather not run their house like a project. We coordinate every vendor, you pay one bill. Memberships start at $199 a month. Adam, who has personally worked in, remodeled, or built about 400 homes in Summit County over 27 years, runs it himself."

### The 30-second vendor pitch

"Members pay HBC monthly. They text us when they need things. We dispatch you. You bill us at your normal rate. No markup, no kickback. We pay you net-15. You get steady, qualified work in a tight geography. Members are upper-middle income, pay for quality, don't haggle."
