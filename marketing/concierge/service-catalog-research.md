# Home Services Concierge — Service Catalog & Vendor Economics
**Research Date:** 2026 | **Geography:** Summit County Ohio (Hudson, Bath Township, Akron suburbs)  
**Home Profile:** Single-family, 2,500–5,000 sqft, 0.25–2 acre lots, full four-season climate

---

## Executive Summary

This document provides HBC with a complete operational and financial blueprint for a residential home services concierge subscription. It covers:

1. A **master catalog of 40+ services** organized by frequency and delivery model
2. A **34-line vendor cost table** grounded in Summit County / Akron-Cleveland market data, with retail benchmarks and estimated wholesale/subcontractor costs
3. A **service-cap definition framework** that eliminates abuse while remaining consumer-friendly
4. A **three-tier recommended launch catalog** ($199 / $350 / $550/month) with clear included, add-on, and excluded rules

**Key economics:** The Akron-Cleveland suburban market sits at mid-range national pricing — roughly 5–10% below Cleveland's urban core and 10–15% below national averages for most trades. A concierge operator using preferred-vendor relationships can negotiate 15–25% sub-retail pricing on recurring services, yielding sustainable gross margins of 25–35% on the service-delivery component before subscription overhead.

**Competitive positioning:** National analog programs such as [Honey Homes](https://www.honeyhomes.com/pricing) price at $2,000–$4,750/year for handyman-hour bundles. [No Worry Home](https://noworryhome.com/pricing/) charges $1,295–$2,995/year for 2,500–5,000 sqft homes with 2–4 preventive visits. HBC's proposed model is differentiated by *broad multi-service bundling* (lawn + cleaning + snow + HVAC + seasonal) rather than handyman hours only — more comparable to a full-service HOA maintenance program sold to individuals.

## How to Define "One Service" (Cap Logic)

### How Competitors Handle It

Most residential concierge operators and industry operators use one of three models:

**Model 1 — Hours-based bank** ([Honey Homes](https://www.honeyhomes.com/pricing), [HandyHomes](https://www.handyhomes.com/pricing))  
Each "visit" is a fixed time block (1 hr 45 min or 2 hrs). Members buy a bank of visits. Any service type can be done; the clock runs. Simple to administer, but doesn't match how homeowners think about "lawn care" as a season-long subscription.

**Model 2 — Maintenance visits** ([No Worry Home](https://noworryhome.com/pricing/))  
2–4 inspection/maintenance visits per year with a technician. Services are bundled around those visits. Does not include recurring services like mowing or cleaning.

**Model 3 — Named-service bundles** (lawn care operators, HOA-style programs)  
Each service is named and frequency-defined. "Lawn mowing" = one service = weekly Apr–Oct, up to 26 cuts. This is the most intuitive framing for consumers but requires explicit frequency definitions.

### Industry-Standard Abuse Vectors

Without clear rules, the most common abuse scenarios are:
1. Claiming "lawn care" should cover aeration, dethatching, overseeding, and mulching under one service slot
2. Treating each snow push as a separate service call and claiming "unlimited" plowing
3. Requesting emergency handyman for routine maintenance items

### Recommended HBC Service-Cap Rules

**Rule 1 — A "service" is a named category with a defined frequency cap.**

> *"Lawn mowing" = 1 service = up to 26 cuts per season (weekly Apr–Oct). 'Lawn treatment' = 1 service = up to 6 applications per year. Each service category has a published annual occurrence cap."*

**Rule 2 — Ancillary tasks within a service visit are included; scope-expansion tasks are not.**

> *Example: Edging, blowing, and line-trimming are included in "lawn mowing." Core aeration, dethatching, overseeding, and sod repair are NOT "lawn mowing" — they are separate add-on services.*

**Rule 3 — Snow plowing counts as one annual "service" with unlimited per-event visits subject to a season trigger.**

> *Snow plowing counts as one service slot. It is included for all push events ≥ 2 inches of snowfall accumulation, up to 30 push events per season. Events beyond 30 or pushes for less than 2 inches are billed at the standard per-push rate.*  
> *Why 30: Summit County averages 20–28 plowable events in a normal winter; capping at 30 protects against extraordinary winters while covering 95%+ of seasons.*

**Rule 4 — On-demand services are never "included" in the service count; they are always extra-cost with a member discount.**

> *Handyman, plumbing, electrical, and tree work are charged at preferred-vendor rates. The member discount (15% off retail) is the benefit — not free inclusion.*

**Rule 5 — Emergency dispatch is coordination-only at no extra charge; trade labor is billed to the homeowner.**

> *HBC dispatches the vendor within agreed response windows. The homeowner is billed directly by the vendor at the negotiated preferred rate.*

**Rule 6 — Published service list governs. Ambiguity resolved in favor of the defined scope.**

> *If a requested task is not in the defined scope of a named service, HBC provides a quote for the add-on. The concierge manager has authority to make one "goodwill" scope exception per member per year.*

**Recommended cap language for member agreement:**

> *"Each named service in your plan is defined by its standard scope and annual occurrence limit as published in the Service Catalog. Visiting your home does not entitle you to any service beyond the defined scope. HBC will notify you before any out-of-scope work is performed and provide pricing in advance."*

## Notes on Seasonality (Summit County Climate Calendar)

Summit County Ohio sits in the eastern Great Lakes climate zone, characterized by lake-effect snow influence from Lake Erie (45 miles north) and full four-season temperature swings. HBC's scheduling calendar should be locked to this rhythm:

| Month | Primary Services | Secondary / Setup |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **January** | Snow plowing (peak), holiday light removal | Pre-season vendor contracting |
| **February** | Snow plowing | Customer renewal outreach |
| **March** | Snow plowing (tail end; last push typically mid-March) | Spring service scheduling |
| **April** | Spring lawn cleanup, mulching, shrub trim (early), sprinkler startup | AC tune-up scheduling, bed edging |
| **May** | Lawn mowing begins (typically Apr 15–May 1), lawn treatment app 1, HVAC AC tune-up | Gutter cleaning (spring), pest control Q2 |
| **June** | Lawn mowing (weekly), lawn treatment app 2 | Power washing (dry weather window), window cleaning |
| **July** | Lawn mowing (weekly), lawn treatment app 3 | Mid-season sprinkler check |
| **August** | Lawn mowing (weekly; growth slows late Aug), lawn treatment app 4 | |
| **September** | Lawn mowing tapering, furnace tune-up, chimney sweep, pest control Q3 | Fall scheduling push |
| **October** | Leaf cleanup begins, lawn mowing final cuts, sprinkler blowout, dryer vent cleaning | Gutter cleaning (fall), roof inspection |
| **November** | Leaf cleanup (peak: first 3 weeks), holiday light installation, last gutter clean | Sump pump check, water heater flush |
| **December** | Snow plowing begins (~Dec 1 typical first push), holiday lights active | Season-end vendor payments |

**Critical scheduling windows:**

- **Leaf cleanup:** The Oct 15 – Nov 20 window is the single most congested service period. Summit County's hardwood canopy (oak, maple, ash) creates extremely heavy leaf load. HBC must pre-position leaf cleanup crews — this is not a schedulable-on-demand service. Route density is critical: plan leaf runs by neighborhood cluster.

- **Snow plowing:** Lake-effect events can deliver 6–12" overnight with little warning. HBC must have sub-vendor contracts signed by October 1 with guaranteed capacity. Consider a per-season retainer + per-push pricing with subs rather than per-push only, to ensure capacity.

- **Spring compression:** The Apr 15 – May 15 period is extremely compressed: sprinkler startup, spring cleanup, mulch, lawn treatment app 1, gutter clean, AC tune-up, and first mows all want to happen simultaneously. Stagger scheduling by neighborhood cluster or property type to avoid bottlenecks.

- **HVAC windows:** AC tune-ups must complete before Memorial Day (before cooling season). Furnace tune-ups must complete before Oct 1 (before first heat demand). Missing these windows creates service failures in emergencies.

**Recommended service scheduling model:**  
Assign each HBC member a "schedule zone" (A, B, or C) staggered over the first 3 weeks of each compressed service window. This distributes vendor load evenly and guarantees each member gets service within the target window rather than on a specific date.
