# REALTOR INTAKE SCRIPT

---

## Opening / First 30 Seconds

*After they say hello:*

"Hey [FirstName], thanks for calling. This is Adam, founder of Hometown Builders Club. I appreciate you taking a few minutes. I'll keep this tight. I want to give you a quick picture of what we do for realtors, and then I'd love to hear a little about your business. Sound good?"

*If they called cold without context:*

"We're a home advisory and trade network out of Cuyahoga Falls. We work with Summit County homeowners before, during, and after a home purchase. We also partner with a small number of local realtors and that's probably why you're calling. So tell me a little about your practice and let's see if this makes sense."

---

## 5 Qualifying Questions

**Q1: How long have you been selling in Summit County, and where are most of your closings?**

- *Good answer:* 3+ years, consistent closings in Summit County towns (Hudson, Bath, Fairlawn, Cuyahoga Falls, Stow, etc.)
- *Flag:* Mostly outside Summit County, or heavily commercial. HBC is residential Summit County only.

**Q2: Roughly how many transactions do you close in a year?**

- *Good answer:* 10 or more. That's the partner threshold. It means they have real volume to refer from.
- *Flag:* Fewer than 6 per year. Not a dealbreaker, but the referral frequency will be low. Be honest about that.

**Q3: What does your post-close process look like right now? Do you stay in touch with buyers after closing?**

- *Good answer:* Yes, some kind of follow-up system. Annual check-in, holiday card, something. Means they care about the relationship.
- *Flag:* "I hand them the keys and move on." That's honest, and they might still be a partner, but the HBC value proposition is strongest for agents who want to stay in the client relationship.

**Q4: When a past client calls you with a contractor question or a repair question, what happens?**

- *Good answer:* They try to help but don't always have a great answer. That's the gap HBC fills.
- *Great answer:* They describe exactly the kind of frustrated "I don't know who to call" situation we solve.
- *Flag:* "I have great contractor relationships already." Note this for the objections section below.

**Q5: Are you familiar with what a Home Clarity Report is, or is this your first time hearing about HBC?**

- *Good answer (either way):* If they know us, ask what they've heard. If they don't, that's perfect. Gives you a clean start.
- *Use this:* Tells you how much ground to cover in the pitch.

---

## The Pitch / 90 Seconds

*Hit all three of these. In this order. Don't rush.*

**1. The Stamp**

"When one of your clients goes through our Home Clarity Report, their home earns the HBC stamp. That stamp travels with the listing. When they sell, you list a home that has documented systems, documented scopes, documented condition. Buyers compare homes. They pick the one with the story. Your listing is that home."

**2. The Hero**

"The other side of it is your past clients. Every call you get from a past buyer that isn't a listing call, a roof question, a contractor question, a weird repair situation, you now have one answer: call Adam. (330) 203-1331. We handle it. Whether or not they buy a Report, they get real help from a licensed remodeler with 27 years in Summit County. You look like the agent who never stopped caring. That's the kind of thing that turns past clients into people who refer you forever."

**3. Either Door**

"And here's what I want to be direct about. We will never recommend another realtor to a homeowner who already has one. If your client comes to us, you stay in the loop. If a homeowner finds us first without a realtor, we send them to a partner from our list. That list is small on purpose. It's the entire reason to be on it."

*Pause. Then:*

"Any of that feel unclear? Or does it make sense so far?"

---

## Common Objections

**Objection 1: "I already have my own contractor connections."**

*Handle it:* "That's good, and it doesn't go away. Most of the realtors we partner with have contractor relationships. What HBC adds is a specific thing you can hand a buyer after closing that has real, documented value. A complete expert assessment of their home, delivered in 5 business days, with a lifetime advisor included. It's a product you can give them, not just a name you can give them. Different category."

**Objection 2: "Why would my clients pay $4,500 for a Report?"**

*Handle it:* "Fair question. Our clients save an average of $16,100 on their first major project because they go in knowing what things actually cost and what they actually need. The $4,500 is the cost of not guessing. For a buyer closing on a $500,000 home in Summit County, it's one of the better decisions they make in the first year of ownership. The realtors who partner with us present it that way, and their clients receive it that way. We're happy to walk you through the full Report so you can see what they're actually getting."

**Objection 3: "What's in it for me, really?"**

*Handle it, directly and without defensiveness:* "Honest answer: a few things. The referral fee is real, we pay on conversions, and the specific number is worth five minutes on a follow-up call. The co-branded marketing materials are real, we produce them at no cost to you and your name is on them. And the harder-to-measure piece is the one that actually compounds: the past clients who remember you as the agent who thought ahead. That's what drives referrals three years from now. All three of those things are part of the partnership from day one."

---

## The Close

*After the pitch and objections:*

"So here's what I'd like to do. Let me set up a 20-minute partnership call with you, no obligation, and I'll walk you through the actual materials, the referral fee structure, and what your first client introduction would look like. If it's not the right fit we'll both know in 20 minutes and I won't waste your time after that."

*If they want to apply now:*

"Great. I'll send you the partner application by email today. Takes about 10 minutes. I'll follow up within 48 hours and we'll go from there."

*If they're not ready:*

"Completely understood. Here's my direct number: (330) 203-1331. When the right client comes along and you wish you had a home expert to hand them, that's the call. I pick up."

# TRADE PARTNER INTAKE SCRIPT

---

## Opening / First 30 Seconds

*After they say hello:*

"Hey [FirstName], appreciate you calling. This is Adam Kilgore, Hometown Builders Club. I'll keep this short. I want to give you a quick picture of how we work and then hear a little about [CompanyName]. Sound good?"

*If they called cold without context:*

"We're a home advisory network in Summit County. We send our homeowners to vetted local trades when a project is ready to go. I'm talking sold jobs, not leads. The homeowner has already agreed to a scope and budget before we make the introduction. That's the model. Let me ask you a few quick questions and we can see if it's a fit."

---

## 5 Qualifying Questions

**Q1: How long has [CompanyName] been operating in Summit County?**

- *Good answer:* 5 or more years. Established enough to have a track record.
- *Flag:* Less than 3 years. Not a disqualifier but means thinner reference base. Be honest: we'll still want to check references.

**Q2: What's your typical project size, and how many jobs do you run at one time?**

- *Good answer:* Aware of their capacity. Can take on one to three HBC referrals per month without overextending.
- *Flag:* Already booked out 6 months with no capacity. Note it. Ask if they want to be on the list for future quarters.

**Q3: Walk me through how you handle communication with a homeowner during a project.**

- *Good answer:* They mention proactive updates, not waiting for the client to chase them. This matters more than almost anything else for HBC homeowners.
- *Flag:* Vague. "We stay in touch." Push gently: "What does that look like in practice?" If they can't answer, that's information.

**Q4: Tell me about your Google reviews. How many do you have and are there any unresolved complaints?**

- *Good answer:* Solid star rating, a handful of reviews or more, no unresolved issues. They know their review profile.
- *Flag:* Haven't looked at their reviews recently, or defensive about a complaint. Ask: "How did that one get resolved?"

**Q5: Are you currently using any lead generation services, like HomeAdvisor or Angi?**

- *Good answer either way:* If yes, great, they know the comparison. If no, they're likely referral-based, which means they already understand quality-over-volume.
- *Use this:* It tells you how to frame the HBC model in the pitch. If they're on HomeAdvisor, the comparison writes itself.

---

## The Pitch / 90 Seconds

*Hit all three of these. In this order.*

**1. Sold Jobs, Not Leads**

"Here's the core thing. We don't send leads. By the time we introduce you to a homeowner, the project scope is written, the budget is approved, and they're ready to sign. You're not bidding the job. You're delivering it. The close rate on our referrals reflects that. It's not 20 percent like HomeAdvisor. It's significantly higher, because the work is mostly done before you arrive."

**2. No Competition, Pricing Already Set**

"You're the one trade we recommend for that project. The homeowner isn't gathering five quotes. They're meeting their contractor. And here's the part that surprises people: the budget they've accepted came from your own pricing data, numbers we pull from you during onboarding. So when you arrive, you're honoring a number that came from you in the first place. No re-negotiation. No awkward day-one surprises."

**3. Permanent Record**

"Every project you finish for an HBC homeowner gets documented in their Home Clarity Hub. Photos, scope, materials, warranty terms, your name. When they sell the house in 10 years, the next buyer sees your work in the listing. Your reputation compounds with every project. Most contractors finish a job and the documentation disappears. Our homeowners carry yours with the house."

*Pause. Then:*

"The fee is 15% on close. No upfront cost. If the job doesn't close, you owe nothing. Any questions before I keep going?"

---

## Common Objections

**Objection 1: "15% feels high."**

*Handle it:* "I hear that a lot, and it makes sense on its face. Here's the comparison that usually shifts it. HomeAdvisor charges $20 to $100 per lead, upfront, whether you close the job or not. You win roughly one in four or five. With HBC, you pay nothing unless the job closes, and the close rate is dramatically higher because the homeowner is already sold. Run the math on what you're spending per closed job right now versus 15% on a sold job that arrived with the scope already written. For most trades we talk to, HBC is cheaper per closed dollar, not more expensive."

**Objection 2: "I get plenty of work already."**

*Handle it:* "That's a good position to be in. Two thoughts. One, the right question isn't volume, it's quality. HBC homeowners come with complete documentation, a signed budget, and a single point of contact. That's a different kind of job than a cold lead. Two, most of the trades in our network treat HBC as a complement to their existing referral base, not a replacement. A handful of sold jobs a year, with the permanent record benefit on top, fits alongside whatever you already have going. But if your calendar is genuinely full and you want to stay in touch for later, that's a real answer too."

**Objection 3: "What if the homeowner backs out after I'm already involved?"**

*Handle it:* "You owe nothing. That's the whole point of the on-close model. We don't introduce you until the homeowner is genuinely ready to move forward. That's not a guarantee against all possible situations, but it's a very different starting point than chasing a lead that was never serious. And if something genuinely unusual happens and a project doesn't close after introduction, we work with you on the next referral. We're not trying to burn partners."

---

## The Close

*After the pitch and objections:*

"Here's what makes sense next. I'll send you the trade partner application. It takes about 10 minutes. We'll schedule a 20-minute follow-up call where I walk you through your first referral, what the documentation package looks like, and how the fee gets handled. After that, if it's a fit, we're in business. If it's not, at least you have the full picture."

*If they want to move fast:*

"I can send the application today. Check your email in the next couple hours. If you get it back to me this week I can turn around a call early next week."

*If they're not ready:*

"Totally fine. Here's my cell: (330) 203-1331. When the calendar opens up, or when a bad HomeAdvisor month makes you reconsider, that's the call to make. I pick up."
