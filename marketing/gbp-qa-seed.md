### Q. What does Hometown Builders Club actually do?

Hometown Builders Club is a home-advisory service for Summit County homeowners and buyers. My job is to give you straight answers about what your home needs, what it will cost, and what order to do it in, so you can make smart decisions without guessing. I also own AK Renovations, so when it is time to build, we can take the work from advice all the way through finished project.

---

### Q. What is the Home Clarity Report and how much does it cost?

The Home Clarity Report is a 2-4 hour walkthrough of your home where I document every repair, update, and renovation it needs, then build a prioritized plan with real cost ranges attached. It costs $4,500. That fee gets applied to your project if you hire AK Renovations for work totaling $25,000 or more. You leave knowing exactly what your home needs and what it will cost.

---

### Q. How is the Home Clarity Report different from a home inspection?

A home inspection tells you what is broken. The Home Clarity Report tells you what to fix, what it will cost, and what order makes sense. Inspectors are looking for defects and writing them up for disclosure. I am looking at the same house through the eyes of someone who has personally remodeled or built about 400 homes in Summit County over 27 years. The output is a plan you can actually act on.

---

### Q. Who should get a Home Clarity Report?

Anyone trying to make a smart decision about a home in Summit County. That includes buyers figuring out whether a house is worth the offer price, homeowners deciding what to tackle before listing, people who just moved in and want a game plan, and homeowners planning a major remodel who want to budget it right before calling a contractor.

---

### Q. What does the Report stamp mean on a real estate listing?

When a seller has completed a Home Clarity Report, I add a stamp to the listing that tells buyers a licensed Ohio GC with 27 years of Summit County experience has walked that home and priced out the work. It gives buyers confidence and helps sellers field cleaner offers. The Report travels with the listing.

### Q. How much does a kitchen remodel cost?

Kitchen projects through AK Renovations typically run $40,000 to $150,000. Where you fall in that range depends on the square footage, whether walls are moving, the cabinet line you choose, appliances, and the condition of your existing plumbing and electrical. The Home Clarity Report will put a specific number on your kitchen before any work starts, so there are no surprises.

---

### Q. How much does a bathroom remodel cost?

Bathroom remodels run $30,000 to $85,000 for most Summit County projects. A straightforward master bath refresh with new tile, fixtures, and vanity sits toward the lower end. A gut renovation with a custom tile shower, heated floors, and relocated plumbing pushes toward the top. We will scope it out precisely before you commit to anything.

---

### Q. How much does a first floor remodel cost?

First floor projects, which often include opening up layouts, refinishing or replacing flooring, updating trim, and sometimes a kitchen component, typically run $50,000 to $150,000. The range is wide because every first floor is different. Schedule a Home Clarity Report and we will put a real number on it.

---

### Q. How long does a kitchen or bathroom project take from start to finish?

A bathroom remodel usually takes 4 to 8 weeks in production once the project starts. Kitchens run 8 to 14 weeks depending on scope and custom cabinet lead times. The planning and selection phase before production starts adds time too. I will give you a realistic schedule at the start, not an optimistic one, because I would rather set the right expectations upfront than apologize later.

### Q. Can the Home Clarity Report help me decide how much to offer on a house?

### Q. What makes Summit County remodeling different from other markets?

The housing stock here is older and more varied than a lot of Ohio suburbs. You have mid-century homes in Cuyahoga Falls, colonial builds from the 1970s and 1980s all over Hudson and Bath, and a mix of everything in between. Lead paint is common. Older plumbing configurations show up regularly. Permit requirements vary by municipality. Twenty-seven years of working in these specific homes means I spot things on a walkthrough that someone newer to this county will miss.
